## Abstract
Powder bed fusion using a laser beam (PBF-LB) involves highly coupled multiphysical phenomena, including melting and solidification, phase transformations, evaporation, buoyancy-driven flow, surface-tension effects, and microstructural evolution. Temporal beam shaping, achieved by modulating the laser power over time, enables precise control of energy distribution, mitigates localized overheating, and enhances print quality. In this work, a multiphysics computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model is developed using the open-source code OpenFOAM to investigate the effects of temporal beam shaping during PBF-LB of 316L stainless steel (316L SS). The numerical model is validated against in-house experimental data by comparing melt-region cross-sectional profiles. To evaluate the influence of temporal beam shaping, we apply pulsed Gaussian lasers with varying duty cycles (10%, 50% and 90%) and analyze their effects on melt-pool behavior. Results reveal that duty cycle variations significantly affect penetration depth due to differences in effective power. At the low duty cycle, the melt pool exhibits pronounced fluctuations in material deposition and transient volume, arising from the partial overlap of discrete melt pools under relatively long cooling durations. In contrast, no fluctuations are observed at relatively high duty cycles, where insufficient cooling time between laser pulses prevents significant temperature changes. This work provides fundamental insights into the effects of process parameters on melt-pool behavior when temporal beam shaping is employed.
